Payment gateway or payment orchestration?

They sound alike and they are often sold together. They do different jobs, and most businesses need the first long before the second.

A MoneyBee guide. Last updated 25 November 2026

The short answer

A payment gateway carries a payment from your checkout to the company that processes it, and brings back the answer: approved or declined. Payment orchestration sits one level above. It connects your checkout to several gateways and providers at once and decides which of them handles each payment.

Every business that takes cards online needs a gateway, or a provider that includes one. Only some need orchestration.

What a payment gateway does

When a customer pays on your website or app, the gateway is the part that collects the payment details on a secure page, sends them to the acquirer or processor, and returns the result to your checkout. In a typical setup it is one connection to one provider's network. Some gateways can also reach more than one processor and offer some routing of their own, so the line between the two is not sharp.

Many providers bundle the gateway with the merchant account, the dashboard and the payouts, so you may never see it as a separate thing. If you signed up with one payment company and it works, you already have a gateway.

What payment orchestration adds

Orchestration does not replace gateways. It manages more than one of them. Your checkout talks to the orchestration layer, and the layer talks to each provider behind it. Compared with a typical single-provider setup, that gives you three things:

  • Choice per payment. Rules send each payment to the provider best suited to it, for example by card type, currency or country.
  • A fallback. If one provider is unavailable, payments can be sent to another, as long as that provider is already connected, onboarded and tested.
  • One view. Payments from every provider appear in one place and one format, which makes reporting and reconciliation simpler.

It also means adding a provider does not mean rebuilding your checkout. It is still work: the layer has to support that provider, you need your own account with it, and the new route should be tested before it carries real payments. Our guide to payment orchestration covers this in more detail.

Do you need both?

If you use orchestration, yes, in the sense that the gateways and providers are still there doing the processing. Orchestration is an extra layer, not a substitute. Who holds your money on its way to you depends on the provider and its licence, not on the label. A gateway or orchestration layer that is only software passes payment instructions and does not handle funds. Many payment companies bundle the gateway with collecting your money and paying it out, so ask each provider who holds the funds before they reach your bank account, and under which licence.

How to tell which one you need

A gateway on its own is usually enough if:

  • You sell in one country, in one currency.
  • Your current provider covers the ways your customers want to pay.
  • An occasional outage is an inconvenience, not a serious loss.

Orchestration starts to earn its place if:

  • You already use two or more providers and maintain a separate integration for each.
  • You are expanding from the UAE into other markets, where one provider rarely covers every country and local payment method equally well.
  • Your volume is large enough that small differences in cost or approval rate between providers add up.
  • Finance spends days each month combining reports from different dashboards.

If none of the second list applies, stay with one good provider. A layer you do not use is cost and complexity with no return. If you are a small business taking payments by link or QR code, you may not have to make this choice yourself, because the gateway is usually built into the tool you use. It is still worth asking which provider sits behind it. A payment link is often a sensible place to start.

Questions to ask a provider that says it does both

  • Is this your own gateway with extra features, or a neutral layer over other providers? Can I bring a provider you do not own?
  • Which providers are connected and live in my market today?
  • Can I set and change the routing rules myself?
  • If your layer is unavailable, can I still take payments?
  • If I leave, what happens to my saved customer payment details and my data?
